# Skoolie Build

&nbsp;

## Foundation and Structure

&nbsp;

The bus frame required assessment for structural integrity before any modifications. Rust inspection on the floor pan revealed surface corrosion in the wheel wells and beneath the rear axle, requiring grinding to bare metal and application of rust converter followed by epoxy primer. The undercarriage was sealed with underbody coating to prevent moisture reinfiltration.

&nbsp;

The existing steel ribs running longitudinally along the interior served as anchor points for the living space layout. These ribs were mapped and their load capacity calculated to determine mounting locations for cabinetry, water systems, and propane infrastructure.

&nbsp;

## Roof and Weatherproofing

&nbsp;

The original bus roof required reinforcement before cutting windows. Steel support beams were welded perpendicular to existing ribs at six-foot intervals to distribute weight loads. Roof penetrations for vents and skylights were sealed using marine-grade butyl rubber and stainless steel fasteners. All seams received multiple coats of polyurethane sealant, applied with precise bead geometry to ensure complete coverage without excess material.

&nbsp;

## Systems Integration

&nbsp;

Water systems utilized 1-inch PEX tubing with crimped brass fittings rated for 100 PSI. Fresh water tank capacity was sized at 75 gallons with separate systems for greywater and blackwater, each fitted with gate valves and clean-out ports. All tanks were secured to the frame using steel brackets with vibration-dampening rubber isolators.

&nbsp;

Electrical architecture employed a 400-amp lithium battery bank with dedicated battery management system monitoring cell voltage and temperature. Wiring followed marine standards with appropriately gauged conductors and marine-grade breakers at each major load point.

&nbsp;

Propane lines used copper tubing with flared connections and a solenoid cutoff valve upstream of the heating system. Pressure was set at 11 inches of water column for all appliances.

&nbsp;

## Climate Control

&nbsp;

Roof insulation consisted of closed-cell spray foam applied to a thickness of 3.5 inches, providing R-21 value while maintaining structural rigidity. Wall cavities were filled with rigid foam board cut to precise measurements and sealed with expanding foam at all edges. Interior wall surface was finished with marine plywood faced with vinyl film rated for moisture resistance.

&nbsp;

The ventilation system incorporated a 12-volt powered roof fan with humidity sensor and a secondary intake vent with motorized damper. Air circulation was designed to prevent condensation buildup through continuous convection paths.

&nbsp;

## Interior Cabinetry

&nbsp;

All cabinetry was built from cabinet-grade plywood with 3/4-inch thickness to withstand road vibration. Joinery used pocket hole construction with structural glue and stainless steel fasteners. Drawer slides were rated for full extension with soft-close mechanisms. Each cabinet assembly was bolted directly to the steel frame ribs using grade 8 bolts and lock washers.

&nbsp;

Countertop material was marine-grade Formica with waterproof substrate backing. All seams were sealed with epoxy sealant to prevent water infiltration. Edge banding was applied to all exposed plywood edges.

&nbsp;

The flooring system used 3/4-inch marine plywood subfloor fastened at 6-inch intervals, covered with vinyl composite flooring rated for RV environments. Transitions between sections were sealed with flexible polyurethane caulk to accommodate thermal expansion.
