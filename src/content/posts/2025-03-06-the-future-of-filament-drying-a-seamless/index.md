# A Custom Solution for the Bambu Printer Eco-System

&nbsp;

One persistent challenge I've faced is the issue of filament moisture, which can lead to a host of problems like stringing, popping, poor layer adhesion, and failed prints. While Bambu Lab, the manufacturer of my printers, hasn't integrated a built-in solution for this, I saw an opportunity to bridge the gap and create a custom drying system.

&nbsp;

The core idea was simple: leverage the existing heat source from a Creality filament dryer and redirect its exhaust directly into the Bambu Lab AMS (Advanced Multi-Swapping) system. This way, the filament would be continuously dried as it was fed into the printer, addressing the moisture issue at the source.

&nbsp;

However, the challenge lay in the fact that these two components were not designed to work together. They were products from different companies with no intention of interoperating. This meant I had to figure out the exact geometries of both units and design custom parts that would seamlessly bridge them.

&nbsp;

I started with a hands-on, iterative approach, using cardboard and blue tape to create templates that captured the precise profiles of the Creality dryer and the Bambu AMS. This allowed me to understand the critical dimensions, angles, and screw boss locations required for a perfect fit. With these templates in hand, I then translated them into a CAD model, where I could refine the design and 3D print the necessary components.

&nbsp;

The final solution involved a three-piece fan assembly that would clip onto the Creality dryer's exhaust, with a flexible, insulated duct running from there into the Bambu AMS. Getting the cleat design right, where it would register against the factory edges without any play, took around 15 printed iterations with sub-millimeter adjustments until the parts seated flush with zero wobble.

&nbsp;

What I particularly enjoyed about this project was the need to understand the injection-molded parts with such precision. There was no room for error – the custom components had to match the factory-made parts exactly, or the whole system wouldn't work. This process of reverse engineering, prototyping, and iterating until I achieved the perfect fit was both challenging and rewarding.

&nbsp;

By redirecting the Creality dryer's exhaust into the Bambu AMS, I was able to create a seamless, integrated solution that keeps the filament dry during the printing process. No added electronics, no new heat source – just a clever repurposing of existing components. This not only solves the filament moisture issue but also demonstrates the power of a maker's mindset, where challenges become opportunities for innovation and customization.
